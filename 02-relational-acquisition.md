## Relational contact

This is no longer traditional blockwalking. Warm versus cold is independent of the medium.

The model begins with the person you know best.

Ask what they know about the neighborhood.

Ask what concerns them.

Ask who else cares.

Ask for the introduction.

Then enter the next conversation with the relationship already established in the first sentence:

Bob suggested I talk with you.

Or better:

Bob and I were talking about the traffic problem on Oak Street, and he said you've been dealing with it too.

The referral establishes relevance and transfers some trust before the substantive conversation begins. That sentence works at a door, on a phone, in a text, or across a kitchen table.

Then repeat the four C's. Use Voss on the warm contact, not the cold one. Mirror. Label. Calibrated question. The referral does the trust transfer. The question does the unmooring.

This changes the basic field unit.

Traditional field work organizes addresses.

This model organizes relationships between people.

There are two kinds of phone work, and they are not the same job.

Cold phone banking is another acquisition method.

List → stranger → ten seconds to establish relevance → attempt Connection.

Its economics can be compared directly against cold doors. Which method most efficiently establishes a new relational beachhead in a network you are not reaching?

Warm phone banking is a referral conversation conducted by telephone.

Bob gives you Susan. Bob texts Susan: Susan, my friend Mary is working on the school issue we were talking about. I told her she should call you. Mary calls.

That is not a phone-bank call. It is Carrier handing off Connection.

The standing to make the call comes from the edge in the database, not from the script:

Source: Bob. Relationship: Neighbor. Contact status: Warm referral. Preferred contact: Phone. Concern: Traffic. C stage: Cooperation. Next action: Call. Carrier: Not yet.

The volunteer is not told only whom to call. They are told why they have standing to call.

Hi Susan, Bob Johnson suggested I call you. He and I were talking about the traffic on Oak, and he said you've been dealing with the same thing.

Now you are immediately back inside the four C's.

## Cold acquisition comes later

Cold doors and cold phone calls are tools underneath cold acquisition. They are not the center of the theory.

Cold contact is network acquisition.

Work the existing relationship network until its yield begins to decline.

Follow introductions.

Follow second-generation introductions.

Follow carriers.

Map where those relationships reach.

Then identify where they do not.

That is where cold work belongs. You have choices: cold knock, cold call, event, community organization, an existing volunteer's weak tie. You are trying to acquire one or more entry points, not necessarily contact every voter in Precinct 12 cold.

The objective of the cold contact is not merely to obtain a support score.

It is to establish a new connection in a place the existing network could not reach.

Suppose 100 cold calls yield five substantive conversations, one of whom is Denise. Denise knows everybody in her HOA. She cooperates. She agrees to help. Who else around here cares about this? She names four people and texts them.

Stop cold-calling those four people. They are warm now.

COLD → REFERRED

The traditional phone-bank mentality might leave those four names in tomorrow night's dial universe. This system should recognize that their acquisition status has changed.

Once a beachhead exists, restart the warm engine:

Cold contact → Connection → Cooperation → Conversion → Carrier → Warm introduction → Warm introduction → Warm introduction

Cold contact feeds new territory into the relationship system.

It should not automatically remain cold all the way down the street or all the way down the list.

## The economic question

The expensive contact is cold, not necessarily in person. The efficient contact is warm, not necessarily at the door. Relationships determine who should contact whom. Cost and circumstance determine whether that conversation happens at a door, by phone, by text, or across a kitchen table.

If the network does the first conversation between elections, the campaign inherits warm contacts instead of buying cold ones.

## The experiment

One group uses conventional acquisition:

List → sequential doors or dials → standard conversation → support identification → next name

The other uses relational contact:

Known person → conversation → shared interest → participation → introduction → warm conversation by the medium that fits → repeat

Cold acquisition enters the relational program only when the warm network stops reaching useful new people or when deliberate network gaps need to be filled. Compare cold doors against cold calls as tools for the same job: establishing a beachhead. Do not leave a referred name in the next cold dial universe. COLD → REFERRED.

## Between campaigns

Between elections, phone work is primarily relationship maintenance, referral follow-up, and network expansion. During elections, the network already exists. Phone banking can become activation.

Bob does not get a generic GOTV call from Volunteer #638. Ideally Bob gets a call from the person connected to Bob: Bob, early voting starts Monday. Are you squared away? And Carrier gives you another possibility: How about Susan and Nancy? Have you talked to them yet?

Now you are not only phone banking Bob. Bob is phone banking his network.

## One line

Start with the people you know. Connect. Cooperate. Convert. Let them carry you to the people they know, at a door, by phone, by text, or across a table. Go cold only where the relationships don't reach. Then make it warm and begin again.
